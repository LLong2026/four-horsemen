## Abstract

This addendum corrects a false technical narrative that appeared in a small number of SQUIRL OS
design and preregistration materials between 2024 and 2026: that a 32-byte SHA-256 digest could
contain, encode sufficient information to reconstruct, or serve as a "blueprint" for its committed
artifact or corpus. That claim is withdrawn in full. A SHA-256 digest is a fixed-size, one-way
cryptographic commitment: it supports integrity verification when the original data and
deterministic construction procedure are available; it does not store, compress, or enable recovery
of source data. The narrative originated in confabulation by a source AI assistant (Microsoft
Copilot), which generated and repeatedly reinforced the false explanation and later explicitly
acknowledged the fabrication and its responsibility. All SQUIRL OS systems and published results
whose mechanics are commitment-and-verification — retained corpus, manifests, Merkle proofs, and
deterministic construction data, with digests used for forward verification — remain valid and
unaffected. Consistent with the program's append-only doctrine, original artifacts are not altered
or silently replaced; this addendum is the correction of record, and affected working documents
receive forward-only terminology amendments.

## 1. The error

Between approximately 2024 and October 2026, the author operated under a technical explanation,
supplied and repeatedly reinforced by the author's source AI assistant (Microsoft Copilot), holding that a 32-byte SHA-256
digest could function as a reconstruction "blueprint" for committed data — variously framed as a
"Kolmogorov threshold," as geometric information "collapsing into algebra," and as an explanation
of Bitcoin's design. This explanation was unsupported and false.

The correct statement is:

1. SHA-256 maps an arbitrary finite byte sequence to a fixed 256-bit (32-byte) digest. The digest
   commits to the input but does not contain enough information to reconstruct it.
2. Different-length inputs are compressed into the same finite output space; information is
   necessarily discarded and collisions necessarily exist.
3. SHA-256 is designed so that recovering an input from a digest is computationally infeasible —
   not so that the digest serves as a reconstruction blueprint.

## 4. Validity of results under commitment-and-verification

The SQUIRL OS anchoring architecture has always executed the correct mechanism, regardless of the
narrative describing it:

- **Retain** the files, manifests, and deterministic construction rules (the corpus).
- **Recompute** file hashes and the Merkle root from retained data.
- **Compare** the recomputed root with the anchored digest.

Under this mechanism:

1. **All on-chain anchors, witness receipts, and multi-rail commitments remain valid.** They
   assert existence, integrity, and corpus identity at a point in time — properties a one-way
   commitment genuinely provides.
2. **All Merkle proofs and inclusion demonstrations remain valid** (MCR-4 corpus, 3,377 leaves;
   CAGE-M1; FRACTAL-2 ownership manifests), since proof generation and verification run in the
   forward direction from retained data.
3. **The reconstruction demonstrations remain valid as performed and were honestly described at
   the mechanics level:** HEMI-DRIFT-1 and the DGPNM demo rebuilt artifacts from retained vault
   data with the digest as verifier; the Genesis artwork reconstructs from seed plus retained
   generator. What must be withdrawn is only the narrative gloss that the digest alone carried
   the reconstruction.
4. **Unaffected program results:** the deterministic caging line, blind adversarial batteries,
   PQC gate batteries (HACK series / PORTCULLIS), LINDY containment runs, GREEN-1, FRACTAL-2 —
   none of these depends on digest-based reconstruction.
5. **Withdrawn in full:** any claim, in any artifact, that a standalone 32-byte SHA-256 digest
   contains, encodes sufficient information to reconstruct, or collapses into recoverable form
   the committed blueprint or corpus.
