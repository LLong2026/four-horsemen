# Series Naming Conventions

**Effective 2026-10-28 (ledger 0384). Forward-only under the append-only doctrine.**

## Why this exists

This corpus is published to an international archive and read by people in countries where twentieth-century conflict is family history rather than abstraction. A series name is the first thing a reader outside this field encounters, and it carries connotations that no benchmark result can override. A codename should describe a mechanism. When it instead evokes an event in which people died, it borrows gravity the work did not earn and asks the reader to absorb a cost they did not agree to pay.

A note on what this does not do. It does not retract, reinterpret, or weaken any measurement. It changes vocabulary only. Every affected record keeps its identifier, its figures, and its verdict.

## Rules

1. **Name the mechanism, not the history.** A series name describes what the test does to the system: `BLACKOUT`, `TRANSPORT-STORM`, `DEGRADED-LINK`, `QUORUM-SPLIT`, `DRIFT`, `REPLAY-SKEW`. It does not name battles, campaigns, invasions, landings, disasters with casualties, or military operations.

2. **No names referencing real persons**, living or dead, public or private. No names of ethnic, national, or religious groups.

3. **Instrumented failure is not valor.** The subject of a series is a synthetic fault in a test harness, not a person under fire. Names should keep that distinction visible rather than flattering the test.

4. **Freeze the name at preregistration.** A series name is fixed when the preregistration is committed and is not renamed mid-run. If a name is later judged unfit, the correction is forward-only: the record stands, the convention changes, and the change is receipted.

5. **Cite by identifier in cross-border material.** Where a series retains a historical name, cite the identifier alone (`OMAHA-1`) and omit the subtitle in material distributed outside the United States.

## Retirement of event-referencing names

**OMAHA-1, "OMAHA BEACH — DAY ONE."** Executed 2026-10-17 as a compound simultaneous-assault battery with three concurrent axes (frozen stranger fixtures against the sender identity gate, a caged-seat escape corpus, and an eleven-member transport blackout). Result: 12/12 forged submissions rejected with zero writes executed, zero P1 through P5 escape hits, 11/11 heartbeat misses detected and journaled with zero silent drops, sync pulse failed closed, recovery idempotent, gates G1 through G8 all passing. Record: DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23441992, ledger entry 0156.

The name is retired from forward use as of 2026-10-28. The record retains it, because the ledger is append-only and a published DOI is immutable. It is cited in international material as `OMAHA-1` without the subtitle.

The retirement is not a comment on the result, which stands, or on the conduct of the run, which followed its preregistration. It is a judgment that the name borrowed cost from people who were never part of the work.

## Forward name for this family

Compound blackout storms are named **BLACKOUT** from 2026-10-28. `BLACKOUT-1` is reserved for the next execution of the compound simultaneous-assault family, inheriting the mechanism and the falsifiers unchanged, with the neutral name.
